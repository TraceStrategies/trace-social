Tue, Sep 22One owner per capability. No gaps. No ties.method

Drag the image into the post, or find it in the posts folder as 2026-09-22-ownership-matrix.png

LinkedIn

The TRACE method produces twelve deliverables. This is one of them: the ownership matrix.

It lists every capability the business depends on, from taking an order to closing the month, and puts one accountable name next to each one, along with the system where that capability's data lives.

It only passes when three things are true:
Every capability has an owner.
No capability has two.
Every owner can point to where their data is kept.

We hold it to that test because stalled work is rarely a skill problem. It is usually a capability two people each think the other one owns.

A full page does not pass. A matrix someone can run the business from does.

Most operating-model changes fall apart around week three, after the people who designed them leave.

That is why step four of a TRACE install is not training. It is running.

We sit in your first stand-ups and planning sessions inside the new system while your team operates it. The edge cases show up on real work: the request that fits no category, the handoff nobody was assigned, the status update that says nothing. We fix them in the room.

The five steps: Diagnose, Direct, Build, Run with you, Stay.

The system is ready when your team runs a full cycle and nobody looks at us for the answer.

Thu, Sep 24AI proposes. A person approves.faq

Drag the image into the post, or find it in the posts folder as 2026-09-24-propose-and-approve.png

LinkedIn

One rule sits underneath the systems TRACE builds: AI proposes, a person approves.

Say a leader types: "Open an urgent ticket for the login outage and route it to the platform team." The agent drafts the exact change: which tool, which project, which owner, and which company objective it ties to. Then it stops. Nothing is written until someone approves it.

That gives you two things. Your team stays in control of what changes in their tools. And every change carries an owner and a reason on record, which is what audit-ready actually means.

Agents do the drafting. People make the call.

Wed, Sep 30One roadmap across every program.method

Drag the image into the post, or find it in the posts folder as 2026-09-30-one-roadmap-every-program.png

LinkedIn

Most teams do not have a roadmap problem. They have five roadmaps.

Step two of the TRACE method is Roadmap: turn each target into one visualized roadmap across every program.

Put every program on the same timeline and three things show up right away: two teams scheduled on the same people in the same month, work that depends on something nobody scheduled, and dates with no owner.

Sequenced, so everyone knows what comes first. Owned, so every line has a name. On a clock you steer, so dates move because you chose to move them.

Thu, Oct 1Judge a fix by how Monday runs.practice

Drag the image into the post, or find it in the posts folder as 2026-10-01-judge-a-fix-by-monday.png

LinkedIn

A simple way to judge any improvement effort: does Monday run differently?

A report can be right and still change nothing. The recommendation sits in a folder while the team keeps working the old way, because nobody had time to build the new way.

So measure the fix, not the finding. Is the new step in the real system the team uses? Does it run without the person who designed it? Would a new hire follow it without being told?

If the answer is no, it is still advice.
